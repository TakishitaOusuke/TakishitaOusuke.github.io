Webflow vs Framer vs Wix Studio: Which Website Builder Is Worth Paying For in 2026?

Disclosure: Some links in this article are affiliate links. If you buy a plan through them, we may earn a commission at no extra cost to you. This article is a research-based comparison of official pricing and documentation — it is not a hands-on review, and we do not claim to have used a paid plan we have not used.

You are down to three website builders and about to pay. At that point, the marketing pages stop helping: all three say they are fast, flexible and easy. What actually decides it is a short list of concrete points — what the first paid plan really costs, whether the CMS fits the site you are building, what happens to your pages and bandwidth as you grow, whether you can take payments, and how hard it is to leave.

This comparison uses only figures published on each vendor's official pricing and help pages, checked in November 2026. Prices are in US dollars and change often, so confirm on the vendor's page before you buy.

The short answer

  • Choose Webflow if your site lives on structured content (blog, portfolio pieces, case studies) and you want the strongest CMS of the three.
  • Choose Framer if you want a design-first portfolio or marketing site live quickly, at the lowest entry price, and you do not need e-commerce.
  • Choose Wix Studio if you need to take payments, bookings or run a small shop inside the same tool, and prefer an all-in-one package over CMS depth.

The 5 points that decide it

1. Do you need a CMS from day one?

Webflow's cheapest plan (Basic) has no CMS at all — you pay $25/mo (Premium) the moment your site has a blog or repeating content. Framer includes a small CMS (2 collections) already in its $10 Basic plan. If your site is five static pages, this difference alone can save you $15 a month.

2. How much content will you publish?

Framer's Basic caps you at 30 pages and 1,000 CMS items; Pro raises that to 150 pages. Webflow Premium allows 20,000 CMS items. For a content-heavy site, Webflow's ceiling is far higher; for a portfolio, Framer's limits rarely bite.

3. Do you need to take payments?

Neither Webflow's Basic plan nor Framer's standard plans are built around a built-in shop checkout for beginners (Webflow sells separate e-commerce plans). Wix Core ($29/mo) is the first Wix tier that accepts payments and adds basic eCommerce and scheduling. If "sell from the site" is a requirement, Wix removes a whole integration step.

4. Design freedom vs. guardrails

Framer is the fastest path from a blank canvas to a polished, animated page — its editor behaves like a design tool. Webflow gives finer control over structure and CMS-driven layouts, at the cost of a steeper learning curve. Wix Studio sits between them: strong templates and AI layout help, less fine control than Webflow.

5. What does year two cost?

All three advertise yearly-billing prices. Paying monthly raises Framer by 50% and Webflow Basic from $15 to $25. Wix's free domain covers year one only on yearly plans. Compare the renewal price, not the first-year headline.

Verdict

For a maker, freelancer or small studio publishing a portfolio plus occasional articles, Framer Basic at $10/mo is the cheapest credible start, and Webflow Premium at $25/mo is the upgrade when the CMS becomes the site. If the site must take money, start with Wix Core at $29/mo instead of bolting payments onto a tool that treats them as an add-on.
